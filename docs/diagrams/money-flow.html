Money flow: funding, payout, collection and settlement

Four legs of client money inside one ring-fenced trust or escrow account, three failure paths, and the platform fee leg kept outside it.

The principle. Client money never sits in the platform's operating account. Every leg goes through a ring-fenced account at a licensed bank: a trust or escrow account, or a virtual account under it. This is the safest design in every country reviewed, and in Zambia and India it also avoids the open question of whether collecting buyer payments counts as payment aggregation.

Legs and failure paths

1. Funding in

The financier sends cash to its escrow or mandated account. Staff record it; the ledger credits that financier's funds.

2. Payout

On acceptance and signature, the financier's cash funds the advance. The seller receives the advance minus the upfront fee into its verified bank account.

Large amounts need a second approver.

3. Collection

The buyer pays the per-invoice collection account named in the notice. Every credit then matches exactly one invoice.

4. Waterfall at maturity

Payment is applied to principal, then discount, then penal charges. Any surplus is paid to the seller; the financier's share goes back to its funds.

F1. Payout fails

Failure path

Retry on the next rail with the same idempotency key.

F2. Buyer short-pays

Failure path

Partial settlement; the rest stays due. With recourse, the shortfall falls to the seller.

F3. Wrong amount or unknown account

Failure path

The credit is held for ops.

Platform fee leg

The seller's payout is the advance minus the upfront fee; the ledger books fees with tax. The guide does not state when or how the earned fee is moved to the platform operating account, so the dashed arrow shows the direction only.
